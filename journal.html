Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks (JERF)

The Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks (JERF) is an open-access scholarly journal associated with the Epistemic Research Frameworks (ERF) Institute. The journal is devoted to conceptual, methodological, and foundational inquiry across disciplines, with particular attention to the structures through which scientific knowledge is produced, organized, interpreted, and evaluated.

JERF provides a venue for research on scientific reasoning, conceptual frameworks, historical and reconstructive inference, theoretical modelling, and related questions concerning the foundations of knowledge. The journal welcomes rigorous work that contributes to the clarification, refinement, and responsible development of scholarly inquiry.

Submission Guidelines

JERF welcomes original scholarly contributions concerned with conceptual, methodological, and foundational questions across disciplines. Submissions must demonstrate intellectual rigor, conceptual precision, clear argumentation, and explicit engagement with relevant evidence, methods, or theoretical frameworks.

The journal does not prioritize disciplinary novelty, publication volume, or academic fashion. Preference is given to work that advances understanding through careful reasoning, methodological transparency, conceptual clarification, or constructive theoretical development.

Authors are expected to write with scholarly discipline, respect for evidence, and awareness of alternative interpretations. Submissions that rely primarily on rhetoric, unsupported speculation, ideological advocacy, or avoidable ambiguity are unlikely to be considered.

Peer Review Process

All submissions undergo an initial editorial evaluation to determine their relevance to the journal's scope, standards of scholarship, and methodological expectations. Manuscripts that do not meet these basic criteria may be declined without external review.

Submissions that pass the initial evaluation are normally reviewed by independent scholars with relevant expertise. Reviews focus on conceptual coherence, methodological rigor, evidential accountability, clarity of argumentation, and engagement with existing scholarship.

Acceptance is not based on agreement with an author's conclusions, disciplinary affiliation, institutional prestige, or theoretical orientation. The primary criterion is whether a submission makes a clear, rigorous, and intellectually responsible contribution to scholarly inquiry.

The Editorial Office reserves the right to request revisions, seek additional evaluations, or decline submissions when significant concerns remain unresolved.

Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance is based on the quality of the contribution rather than its disciplinary affiliation, institutional origin, or theoretical orientation. JERF evaluates submissions according to standards of intellectual rigor, conceptual clarity, methodological transparency, and evidential accountability.

Manuscripts should present a clearly identifiable scholarly contribution, demonstrate familiarity with relevant scholarship, and engage competing interpretations fairly and responsibly. Arguments must be supported by appropriate evidence, reasoning, or methodological justification.

Particular weight is given to originality of insight, coherence of analysis, reproducibility of reasoning, and the capacity of a contribution to clarify, refine, or strengthen existing frameworks of inquiry.

Manuscripts that are primarily rhetorical, polemical, speculative without methodological grounding, or insufficiently supported by evidence are unlikely to satisfy the journal's standards for publication.

Copyright Policy

Authors retain copyright of their work. By submitting and publishing in JERF, authors grant the journal a non-exclusive right to publish, distribute, archive, and preserve the accepted version of the manuscript as part of the scholarly record.

Authors remain free to share, reproduce, and republish their work elsewhere, provided that the original publication in the Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks (JERF) is appropriately acknowledged.

Responsibility for the content of published articles remains with the authors. The views, interpretations, and conclusions expressed in published contributions do not necessarily reflect those of the Editorial Team or the Epistemic Research Frameworks (ERF) Institute.
