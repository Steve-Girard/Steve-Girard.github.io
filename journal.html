Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks (JERF)

The Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks (JERF) is an open-access scholarly journal associated with the Epistemic Research Frameworks (ERF) Institute. The journal is devoted to conceptual, methodological, and foundational inquiry across disciplines, with particular attention to the structures through which scientific knowledge is produced, organized, interpreted, and evaluated.

JERF provides a venue for research on scientific reasoning, conceptual frameworks, historical and reconstructive inference, theoretical modelling, and related questions concerning the foundations of knowledge. The journal welcomes rigorous work that contributes to the clarification, refinement, and responsible development of scholarly inquiry.

Submission Guidelines

JERF welcomes original scholarly contributions concerned with conceptual, methodological, and foundational questions across disciplines. Submissions must demonstrate intellectual rigor, conceptual precision, clear argumentation, and explicit engagement with relevant evidence, methods, or theoretical frameworks.

The journal does not prioritize disciplinary novelty, publication volume, or academic fashion. Preference is given to work that advances understanding through careful reasoning, methodological transparency, conceptual clarification, or constructive theoretical development.

Authors are expected to write with scholarly discipline, respect for evidence, and awareness of alternative interpretations. Submissions that rely primarily on rhetoric, unsupported speculation, ideological advocacy, or avoidable ambiguity are unlikely to be considered.

Peer Review Process

All submissions undergo an initial editorial evaluation to determine their relevance to the journal's scope, standards of scholarship, and methodological expectations. Manuscripts that do not meet these basic criteria may be declined without external review.

Submissions that pass the initial evaluation are normally reviewed by independent scholars with relevant expertise. Reviews focus on conceptual coherence, methodological rigor, evidential accountability, clarity of argumentation, and engagement with existing scholarship.

Acceptance is not based on agreement with an author's conclusions, disciplinary affiliation, institutional prestige, or theoretical orientation. The primary criterion is whether a submission makes a clear, rigorous, and intellectually responsible contribution to scholarly inquiry.

The Editorial Office reserves the right to request revisions, seek additional evaluations, or decline submissions when significant concerns remain unresolved.

Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance is based on the quality of the contribution rather than its disciplinary affiliation, institutional origin, or theoretical orientation. JERF evaluates submissions according to standards of intellectual rigor, conceptual clarity, methodological transparency, and evidential accountability.

Manuscripts should present a clearly identifiable scholarly contribution, demonstrate familiarity with relevant scholarship, and engage competing interpretations fairly and responsibly. Arguments must be supported by appropriate evidence, reasoning, or methodological justification.

Particular weight is given to originality of insight, coherence of analysis, reproducibility of reasoning, and the capacity of a contribution to clarify, refine, or strengthen existing frameworks of inquiry.

Manuscripts that are primarily rhetorical, polemical, speculative without methodological grounding, or insufficiently supported by evidence are unlikely to satisfy the journal's standards for publication.

Copyright Policy

Authors retain copyright of their work. By submitting and publishing in JERF, authors grant the journal a non-exclusive right to publish, distribute, archive, and preserve the accepted version of the manuscript as part of the scholarly record.

Authors remain free to share, reproduce, and republish their work elsewhere, provided that the original publication in the Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks (JERF) is appropriately acknowledged.

Responsibility for the content of published articles remains with the authors. The views, interpretations, and conclusions expressed in published contributions do not necessarily reflect those of the Editorial Team or the Epistemic Research Frameworks (ERF) Institute.

Publication Timelines

JERF seeks to provide timely editorial decisions while maintaining rigorous scholarly standards. Because each submission is evaluated on its intellectual and methodological merits, review times may vary according to the complexity of the manuscript and the availability of qualified reviewers.

The journal does not operate under accelerated publication targets. Editorial decisions are guided by the quality of evaluation and review rather than by predetermined production schedules. Authors will be informed of significant developments throughout the review process.

As an annual publication, JERF prioritizes careful review, constructive feedback, and editorial consistency over publication volume. The journal aims to balance efficiency with the deliberative standards expected of serious scholarly inquiry.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions below summarize a number of issues frequently encountered during the preparation and evaluation of scholarly work. They are provided to clarify the journal's expectations regarding rigor, methodology, submission practices, and the broader aims of the editorial process.

May authors submit work from any discipline?
Yes. JERF evaluates submissions according to their conceptual, methodological, and scholarly quality rather than their disciplinary origin. Contributions may come from the natural sciences, social sciences, humanities, interdisciplinary research, or other domains, provided they engage questions relevant to the journal's scope.

Does JERF favor particular theoretical perspectives?
No. The journal does not endorse specific schools of thought, methodologies, or theoretical traditions. Manuscripts are evaluated on the clarity of their arguments, the rigor of their reasoning, and the quality of their scholarly contribution.

Must submissions present new empirical results?
No. JERF welcomes conceptual analyses, methodological investigations, theoretical contributions, historical reconstructions, and other forms of scholarship that advance understanding without necessarily introducing new empirical data.

How long should submissions be?
There is no strict word limit. Authors are encouraged to write with precision, clarity, and appropriate scholarly restraint. Length should be justified by the demands of the argument rather than by convention.

Are interdisciplinary submissions encouraged?
Yes. JERF welcomes contributions that cross disciplinary boundaries when such crossings contribute to conceptual clarity, methodological understanding, or the development of coherent frameworks of inquiry. Interdisciplinarity is valued when it strengthens rigor rather than replaces it.

May authors challenge established theories or prevailing interpretations?
Yes. Critical examination of existing frameworks is a legitimate and often necessary part of scholarly inquiry. However, criticism should be supported by clear reasoning, appropriate evidence, and constructive engagement with relevant scholarship.

Does JERF publish negative results or unresolved questions?
Yes. Contributions that clarify the limits of current knowledge, identify methodological obstacles, or expose unresolved conceptual difficulties may be valuable when presented with rigor and scholarly discipline.

Is open disagreement between authors permitted?
Yes. Scholarly disagreement is an essential component of intellectual progress. JERF encourages respectful and evidence-based engagement with competing arguments, interpretations, and theoretical positions.

What distinguishes a publishable article from an interesting idea?
An interesting idea becomes a publishable contribution when it is developed through explicit reasoning, methodological transparency, engagement with relevant scholarship, and a clear demonstration of its scholarly significance.

What is the journal's position on speculative work?
Speculative ideas may be considered when their assumptions are clearly identified, their reasoning is explicit, and their relationship to existing evidence or theory is responsibly addressed. Speculation alone does not constitute a scholarly contribution.

Can independent scholars submit manuscripts?
Yes. Manuscripts are evaluated according to scholarly quality rather than institutional affiliation. Independent researchers are held to the same standards of rigor, evidence, and methodological accountability as all other contributors.

Call for Contributions

JERF welcomes contributions from scholars whose work engages conceptual, methodological, and foundational questions relevant to the production, interpretation, and evaluation of knowledge. The journal is particularly interested in manuscripts that clarify, challenge, refine, or strengthen existing frameworks of inquiry through rigorous reasoning and methodological transparency.

Authors from all disciplines, institutional affiliations, and scholarly traditions are encouraged to contribute. Particular value is placed on conceptual precision, evidential accountability, intellectual discipline, and a willingness to engage difficult questions with scholarly seriousness.

Editorial Perspective

The Journal of Epistemic Research Frameworks is founded on a simple premise: the quality of knowledge depends not only on the results that inquiry produces, but also on the conceptual and methodological conditions that make those results meaningful. JERF exists to support careful scholarship devoted to the examination, clarification, and refinement of those conditions.

The journal values intellectual rigor over immediacy, conceptual precision over novelty alone, and disciplined reasoning over rhetorical persuasion. Its purpose is not to maximize publication volume, but to provide a stable venue for contributions that strengthen the foundations of scholarly inquiry and encourage thoughtful engagement across disciplinary boundaries.
